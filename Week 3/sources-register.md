## 3. Classification notes

- The embedded listing data is **pure operational data** — the product uses it to produce output (card lists, calculation results); it does **not** serve as evidence that the underlying problem is real.
- The 10–15%/year home-price-growth article, the floating-rate back-calculation, and the closing-cost legal citations (§1) are all **assumption-justification evidence** (a narrow form of problem evidence) — they justify picking specific assumption numbers, they do not demonstrate that users struggle to compare BUY vs. RENT.
- The 4 sources in §2 are the **real problem evidence**: they show (a) home prices in Hanoi far outpace income (VietnamPlus/CBRE, VnExpress), and (b) young people are already forced to improvise a buy-vs-rent strategy without a supporting tool (VnEconomy/Happiness Saigon) — exactly the gap FinFlow fills.
- **Shared limitation across all 4 sources:** none specifically addresses Cau Giay, or the specific behavior of "comparing BUY vs. RENT costs using a calculator." This remains one inferential step by the team (high home prices + young people shifting to renting ⇒ they need to compare the two options). A short survey or 5–10 quick interviews would strengthen this with more direct evidence if time allows.
