# Finvlow
# Buy or Lease

## 1. Project Overview

Buy or Lease is a proposed financial decision-support web tool designed to help young households and first-time homebuyers compare the long-term financial outcomes of buying a home versus continuing to lease.

The project focuses on a common but financially complex household decision. Rather than considering only the monthly mortgage payment or rent, the project aims to account for multiple financial factors over time, including household income, house price, rent, mortgage interest rate, loan repayment period, expected house-price appreciation, inflation, and potential investment returns.

The project is currently in the problem-definition stage. The Week 1 objective is therefore to identify and refine the target customer, understand their pain point, and define the financial decision that the future product could support.

## 3. Initial Problem Candidates

During Week 1, the group considered three potential project directions rather than immediately selecting a single solution.

**Candidate 1 — Common Spending Pot for Group Travel**

The first idea was to build a tool that allows a group of friends travelling together to create and manage a shared spending pot.
The tool would aim to make group expenses more transparent and easier to manage by allowing users to contribute money to a common pool and track spending from that pool.

Potential problem:
Groups travelling together may find it difficult to manage shared expenses transparently and keep track of who contributes to and spends from the common fund.

**Candidate 2 — Financial Life Simulation Game**

The second idea was to build a life simulation game in which players make different financial decisions and observe their consequences.
The initial concept aimed to use gameplay to educate users about financial decision-making and the risks associated with different choices.

Potential problem:
Users may have difficulty understanding the long-term consequences and risks of financial decisions because these consequences are often difficult to experience or visualize in real life.

**Candidate 3 — Buy or Lease**

The third idea was to build a web-based tool that helps users compare the financial consequences of buying a home versus continuing to lease.
The tool would consider household income, house price, rent, mortgage interest rates, loan repayment period, expected house-price appreciation, inflation, and potential investment returns.
The leasing scenario would also consider the potential investment opportunity of capital that would otherwise be committed to purchasing the property.

Potential problem:
Young households and first-time homebuyers may find it difficult to determine whether buying or leasing is financially preferable because the decision requires them to compare multiple financial factors and their long-term consequences simultaneously.

## 4. Evaluation of the Problem Candidates

The group evaluated the three candidates using the five-filter framework: **Specific, Relevant, Meaningful, Supportable, Feasible.**

| Filter | Candidate 1: Common Spending Pot | Candidate 2: Financial Life Game | Candidate 3: Buy or Lease |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Specific** — clear target user, clear task/decision | Moderate | Low | High |
| **Relevant** — genuinely a finance/banking problem | Moderate | High | High |
| **Meaningful** — has real differentiation and value | Low (similar functions already exist in apps such as MoMo and Timo) | Moderate (educational value hard to define/measure) | Higher (clear, measurable financial outcome) |
| **Supportable** — clear inputs, rules, or calculations technology can act on | Moderate | Moderate | High |
| **Feasible** — realistic within team capacity and timeline | High | Moderate | High |
| **Overall direction** | Rejected | Rejected | **Selected** |

**Candidate 1 — Reason for Rejection**
The group considered the common spending pot idea relatively simple and found that similar functions are already available through existing financial and digital payment applications, including services such as MoMo and Timo. As a result, the group considered that the project would have limited differentiation and that its potential added value would be relatively low compared with the other candidates.

**Candidate 2 — Reason for Rejection**
The financial life simulation game initially lacked a sufficiently specific target user and a clearly defined user outcome. The group also found it difficult to identify exactly what aspect of financial education the game would quantitatively or meaningfully address. Without a clearly defined financial learning objective and measurable output, there was a risk that the project would become primarily an entertainment-oriented simulation game rather than a solution to a specific financial problem.

**Candidate 3 — Reason for Selection**
The Buy or Lease concept provides a clearer financial decision and allows the group to define a specific target customer and measurable financial outcomes. The decision also involves multiple financial concepts, including mortgage financing, investment returns, opportunity cost, inflation, housing appreciation, and long-term wealth accumulation. Therefore, the group selected Candidate 3 as the initial project direction.

## 5. Target Customer

The current target customer is:

> Young households and first-time homebuyers who are considering whether to purchase a home or continue leasing.

The group selected this target customer because buying a home is a significant long-term financial decision. First-time buyers may need to evaluate multiple financial variables simultaneously and may not have sufficient experience to assess the long-term consequences of the alternatives.

Checked against the four target-user criteria (Specific, Relevant, Reachable, Task-based):
- **Specific** — partially met. "First-time homebuyer" narrows the group, but "young households" is still broad and may need further narrowing (e.g., by life stage, income bracket, or city).
- **Relevant** — met; this group directly faces the buy-vs-lease decision.
- **Reachable** — to be confirmed in Week 2 through interviews, surveys, or existing consumer research, since the group has not yet validated that this segment is observable/interviewable within project constraints.
- **Task-based** — met; the segment is defined by an active decision (buy vs. lease), not only by demographics.

Based on the Week 1 checkpoint feedback, the target customer and their actual pain point require further investigation and validation. The current target customer is therefore considered a working hypothesis rather than a fully validated customer segment.

## 6. User Decision

The primary decision that the future product aims to support is:

> Should a young household or first-time homebuyer buy the property they are considering, or continue leasing and invest the capital that would otherwise be used for purchasing the property?

The decision requires comparison of two alternative financial paths.

**Buying**
The user would evaluate factors such as:
- House price
- Down payment
- Mortgage amount
- Mortgage interest rate
- Loan repayment period
- Mortgage payments
- Expected house-price appreciation
- Inflation
- Potential maintenance costs
- Remaining mortgage balance

**Leasing**
The user would evaluate:
- Monthly or annual rent
- Changes in rent over time
- Capital that would otherwise be committed to the property purchase
- Potential investment return from that capital
- Potential difference between the financial outcomes of leasing and buying

The exact variables and assumptions will be refined in subsequent weeks.

## 7. User Pain Point / Difficulty

The current hypothesis is that the target customer faces difficulty because the buy-versus-lease decision cannot be evaluated effectively by looking at only one variable, such as monthly rent or mortgage payments.

The decision involves multiple interconnected financial variables whose effects occur at different points in time. For example, a household needs to consider:
- The upfront capital required to purchase a property;
- The cost of borrowing;
- The opportunity cost of the capital used for the purchase;
- Future rental payments;
- Potential investment returns;
- Changes in property value;
- Inflation;
- The duration for which the household expects to remain in the property; and
- Other potential ownership costs.

The group therefore identifies the following preliminary pain point:

> Young households and first-time homebuyers may struggle to compare the long-term financial consequences of buying versus leasing because multiple financial costs, returns, and opportunity costs must be evaluated simultaneously over time.

This pain point remains a hypothesis that the group needs to investigate further.

## 8. Draft Problem Statement

> Young households and first-time homebuyers may struggle to determine whether buying or leasing a home is financially preferable because the decision requires them to evaluate multiple short- and long-term financial factors simultaneously, including housing prices, rent, mortgage costs, investment returns, inflation, and expected changes in property value.

The problem statement deliberately focuses on the user and their difficulty, rather than specifying the web tool as the solution.

## 9. Why This Is a Financial Problem

The problem is directly related to personal and household financial decision-making. The comparison between buying and leasing involves several core financial concepts:

**Time Value of Money** — Cash flows associated with buying and leasing occur at different points in time. Comparing the alternatives therefore requires consideration of the time value of money.

**Mortgage Financing** — Buying a property commonly involves borrowing. The interest rate, loan amount, and repayment period affect the household's future cash flows and total financing cost.

**Opportunity Cost** — Capital used for a down payment or property purchase cannot simultaneously be invested elsewhere. The potential return from investing that capital represents an important opportunity cost.

**Investment Returns** — Under the leasing scenario, capital that would otherwise be committed to purchasing the property may potentially be invested. The resulting investment return affects the long-term financial outcome of leasing.

**Housing Appreciation** — Changes in property value affect the eventual financial position of a household that purchases a home.

**Inflation** — Inflation can affect both housing-related costs and the real value of future cash flows.

**Long-Term Wealth Accumulation** — The final comparison is not limited to monthly affordability. It considers how the two alternatives may affect the household's financial position over a specified period.

## 10. Product Output — Supportability Check (Detailed Design Deferred to Week 2)

Per the Week 1 checkpoint feedback, the group is deliberately **not** finalizing the exact output, formulas, or calculation methodology at this stage — doing so before the target customer and pain point are validated would mean choosing a solution ahead of the problem, which the group wants to avoid.

Instead, this section only records the **Supportable** check: whether the problem is realistically the kind that technology can act on. The group finds the buy-vs-lease decision supportable because:
- It has clear numeric inputs (income, price, rent, rate, term, appreciation, inflation, etc.);
- It requires a rule-based comparison between two financial paths rather than open-ended judgment;
- Its output can be expressed as a measurable financial figure rather than a subjective opinion.

At a high level (not finalized), the future product is expected to compare, rather than simply issue a "BUY" or "LEASE" label:

- **Buy path** — the household's financial position after a period, considering property value, remaining debt, and relevant ownership costs.
- **Lease path** — the household's financial position considering the invested value of capital that would otherwise have gone to the purchase, net of accumulated leasing costs.

The exact variables, assumptions, calculation methodology, and financial metrics are intentionally left undefined here and will only be developed after the target customer and pain point are sufficiently validated (see Section 15 — Week 2 Direction).

## 11. Open Questions

The group has identified several questions that require further investigation:
- Is young households sufficiently specific as a target customer, or should the segment be narrowed further?
- What specific characteristics define a first-time homebuyer for the purpose of this project?
- What is the most important pain point experienced by the target customer?
- Do potential users currently compare buying and leasing using the factors identified by the group?
- Which financial variables are most important to the target customer's decision?
- What time horizon should be used for the comparison?
- How should investment returns be incorporated into the leasing scenario?
- Should maintenance costs be included?
- How should house-price appreciation and rent growth be estimated?
- How should inflation affect the comparison?
- What financial metric should ultimately be used to compare the two outcomes?
- How can the group validate that the tool addresses a real customer pain point rather than simply performing a theoretical financial calculation?

## 12. Checkpoint Feedback

Feedback
The group received the following feedback during the Week 1 checkpoint:

The group should focus more on identifying the target customer and understanding their pain point.

Interpretation
The feedback indicated that the group had developed the product concept relatively quickly but had not yet sufficiently validated:
who specifically experiences the problem;
what the customer's actual difficulty is;
how the customer currently approaches the decision; and
which part of the decision creates the greatest difficulty.

The feedback therefore shifted the group's attention from developing the tool itself toward understanding the customer and problem first.

## 13. Revision After Checkpoint

Following the checkpoint, the group refined the project direction.

Before Feedback
The initial concept was primarily framed as:
A tool that calculates whether someone should buy or lease a house.

After Feedback
The group reframed the problem around:
Young households and first-time homebuyers who face difficulty comparing the long-term financial consequences of buying versus leasing.

The group also recognized that the target customer and pain point have not yet been fully validated.

Therefore, the next stage of the project will focus on investigating the target customer's actual needs, decision-making process, and financial pain points before finalizing the product design.

## 14. Week 1 Decision

**Checkpoint result: Keep**

The group is keeping the current problem direction (Buy or Lease) rather than changing, simplifying, or restarting it. The checkpoint feedback did not challenge the direction itself, but the depth of customer/pain-point validation behind it — so the group continues with Buy or Lease while conducting further research into the target customer and their pain point.

Selected direction: BUY OR LEASE
The group will not treat the current assumptions about young households and first-time homebuyers as final until they have been further investigated.

## 15. Week 2 Direction

The next stage will focus on moving from a preliminary problem hypothesis toward a better-supported problem definition.

The group will:
- Investigate the characteristics of young households and first-time homebuyers.
- Identify their actual concerns when deciding whether to buy or lease.
- Investigate how they currently make this decision.
- Identify which financial variables they consider.
- Determine which aspects of the decision are most difficult to compare.
- Refine the target customer if necessary.
- Refine the problem statement based on evidence.
- Begin defining the financial logic and potential product direction only after the customer problem is sufficiently clear.

## 17. Conclusion

During Week 1, Group 4 considered three different financial project directions and selected Buy or Lease as the most promising problem direction.

The selected problem focuses on the difficulty faced by young households and first-time homebuyers when comparing the long-term financial consequences of buying a property with those of continuing to lease and investing available capital.

The group has identified a preliminary target customer, user decision, pain point, and financial rationale. However, following checkpoint feedback, the group recognizes that these elements require further validation.

Therefore, the main objective moving forward is not simply to build a calculator, but to first establish a sufficiently clear understanding of who the target customer is, what their actual pain point is, and why the buy-versus-lease decision is difficult for them.
